01 — The premise Why exams break

Three failures. One root cause: the secret is kept too long, by too many.

Failure 01

The paper exists before the exam

A printed paper is a valuable secret held by presses, couriers, strongrooms and centre staff for weeks. A leak is not an anomaly in that design — it is the design working exactly as built.

We fix it Nothing to steal. Papers are assembled per candidate at T−0 inside a sealed pipeline, and the ciphertext is useless until identity clears.

Failure 02

Identity is checked once, at a desk

A photograph, a signature, a tired invigilator at 9 a.m. Proxy substitution survives because verification is treated as a moment rather than a continuously held state.

We fix it Two cameras, one subject, cross-matched frame by frame — and a sealed chip proving the frames came from real hardware.

Failure 03

Integrity is reported as a score

Remote proctoring returns a probability — "87% suspicious" — and a career turns on it. A candidate cannot cross-examine a number, and a court cannot audit one.

We fix it No risk scores. Timestamped, deterministic event records; three permitted verdicts; a named human on every flag.

02 — The model Chain of custody

Three locks in series. The paper is the last thing to open.

Each lock is independently verifiable and independently auditable. Breaking one does not weaken the others — it stops the session.

Lock 01 · Module A

Identity lock

Government ID parsed on-device, active liveness, and a second camera at 60°–120° whose head-motion vector must correlate with the first in real time.

CORR ≥ 0.94 Held
Lock 02 · Module B

Display lock

The server paints a 3-second token on the exam screen. The phone reads it and reports back over an independent channel. Arrive late, or point at a decoy, and the lapse is recorded.

WINDOW 2000 MS Held
Lock 03 · Module C

Paper lock

Encrypted item bank, opened only after locks one and two hold. The generator then re-parameterises every item for that candidate — while the IRT calibrator holds difficulty constant.

RELEASE AT T−0 Held

Single subject

Document parsing on device

Aadhaar, passport or driving licence OCR runs client-side; only the extracted field hashes and a match decision leave the machine.

Active liveness, not passive

Randomised prompts — rotate to 45°, show the left ear, follow the dot — scored against a 468-point facial mesh and depth symmetry.

Hardware root of trust

The phone app signs every media payload with Apple App Attest or Android Play Integrity. Emulators, rooted devices and virtual cameras fail the chain, not the model.

Angle enforcement

The rig must sit between 60° and 120° of the primary axis. Outside that window the session cannot start — geometry is a precondition, not a score.

    Independent channel

    The read-back travels over the phone's own WebSocket, not the laptop's. A compromised exam machine cannot forge its own confirmation.

    Server clock is authoritative

    Validity is measured against the server's own NTP-disciplined clock, so client clock drift or replay buys an attacker nothing.

    Decoys collapse immediately

    A wall, a photograph or a second monitor produces a stale token or none at all. Either way the mismatch is written to the record within one cycle.

    Lapse ≠ accusation

    A lapse is an event, not a verdict. It routes the session to a human reviewer with the exact clip and timestamps attached.

    Names, places, numeric parameters, option order and phrasing all move. The difficulty, the cognitive operation and the expected solve time do not.

    Zero-leakage delivery gate

    Papers stay encrypted server-side. The decryption key is derived only after Level 3 identity and screen-binding both hold, and is never persisted on the client.

    Late arrivals are covered

    A candidate who starts twenty minutes late gets a freshly generated paper, not a leftover one. There is no window in which an earlier paper helps a later candidate.

    Generation is bounded

    The model rewrites within a template's declared parameter ranges and answer-preserving transforms. It does not invent physics, and every variant is machine-solved before release.

    Parity is enforced, not hoped for

    Variants outside the ±0.05 logit band are rejected by the calibrator and never reach a candidate. See the curves ↓

    Deterministic evidence and human-in-the-loop review

    Module D

    No integrity scores. No automated rejections. A machine may only observe and record; a trained human decides, and the candidate can read the same record the reviewer read.

      Exactly three verdicts

      Clear, Review required, Not assessable. There is no fourth outcome, and "Not assessable" always carries a remedy — a free re-sit, never a penalty.

      Reviewers are independent

      Reviewers are not employed by the institution whose exam they are reviewing, are randomly assigned, and see the evidence before they see the candidate's name.

      Every record is reproducible

      Given the same inputs the engine emits byte-identical events. A tribunal can replay a session and get the same trail three years later.

      Appeals are first-class

      A candidate can open a dispute from inside their own report. The clock on evidence retention pauses for the duration of the appeal.

      04 — Deployment Three levels of assurance

      Match the stakes. Nothing heavier, nothing lighter.

      An institution declares the level; the candidate sees the level before they consent; the certificate records the level it was earned under.

      Level 1 Low stakes

      Browser & webcam

      • Single-camera identity check at start
      • Static item bank with option shuffling
      • Event log, no continuous media retention

      Formative assessment, practice tests, internal coursework.

      Level 2 Medium stakes

      Dual camera, browser + app

      • Dual-angle spatial verification
      • Screen-binding token loop
      • JIT variant generation with IRT parity
      • Human review on every flag

      University entrance tests, hiring assessments, structured interviews.

      Level 3 National scale

      Dual camera + attested status agent

      • Everything in Level 2, plus
      • Sealed-chip attestation on every media payload
      • Lightweight system status agent — display topology, virtual-camera and remote-desktop detection
      • Independent reviewer pool with agency observer access

      NEET, JEE, UPSC, banking recruitment and state common entrance tests.

      The status agent reports state, not content. It has no keylogger, reads no files, and inspects no other application's memory — it answers a fixed set of questions about displays, capture devices and remote sessions, and it uninstalls itself when the exam ends.

      06 — Reality Edge-case protocol

      The exam is at home. Home is not a controlled environment.

      Every one of these has a defined, candidate-favourable protocol. None of them ends a career by accident.

      E01 The internet drops mid-paper

      Answers are already sealed locally. The timer halts within 400 ms, the client retries for 15 minutes, and on reconnect the lost seconds are credited back in full. The gap is an event on the record, never a verdict.

      E02 The phone rings

      The exam app holds the camera session through an incoming call and the timer pauses. A call answered is logged as a stream interruption and routed to human review — not scored, not failed.

      E03 The phone battery dies

      Charging is mandatory at Level 3 and warned at 30%. On loss, the candidate has 10 minutes to restore the secondary angle; beyond that the session becomes Not assessable with an automatic free re-sit.

      E04 The mount slips or the phone falls

      Geometry loss is detected within two frames. The candidate is prompted to re-seat the device, and the reposition window is timed out of the exam, not out of the candidate.

      E05 Mains power fails

      The client runs from battery and degrades to audio-plus-low-frame-rate video at 15% charge. Region-wide outages are detected across sessions and escalate to an agency-level reschedule.

      E06 Someone walks into the room

      A second face is recorded as an environmental event with the clip attached. A family member crossing the frame and a second person seated at the desk are different facts, and a human decides which happened.

      E07 A candidate needs a comfort break

      Scheduled and medical breaks are declared in advance and honoured. Re-entry requires a fresh liveness check; break time is excluded from the exam clock by policy.

      E08 The candidate uses assistive technology

      Screen readers, magnification and scribes are first-class configurations, declared at registration. The status agent allowlists them; extra time is applied to the generated paper's timing model.

      E09 The candidate has one device, not two

      Level 3 requires two. Where a second device is unavailable, the agency assigns a supervised civic node — a library or panchayat centre with loan hardware — so the level is never a wealth test.

      07 — Fairness Psychometric parity

      Different papers are only fair if they are the same test.

      Item Response Theory turns that from a promise into a measurement. Every generated variant carries the same three parameters as its parent — discrimination, difficulty and guessing — inside a tolerance the calibrator enforces before release.

      Item characteristic curves — one item, eight variants

      3PL model: P(θ) = c + (1 − c) / (1 + e−1.7a(θ − b)). The shaded envelope is the ±0.05 logit parity band; a variant that leaves it is rejected before delivery.

      PHY-1104
      Base item, calibrated (dotted) AI-generated variants, n = 8 (solid) ±0.05 logit parity envelope
      View as table

      Difficulty parity across a delivered cohort

      Twelve variants of PHY-1104 as delivered on 30 July. Every b-parameter sits inside the tolerance band.

      Base item b = 0.380 Delivered variant Tolerance ±0.05

      What the calibrator holds constant

      • a — SLOPE Discrimination. How sharply the item separates candidates near its difficulty. Held to ±0.01.
      • b — LOCATION Difficulty on the ability scale. Held to ±0.05 logits — the visible band above.
      • c — ASYMPTOTE Guessing floor. Fixed by option count and distractor plausibility, so variants may not change how many options there are.
      • t̄ — TIME Expected solve time from the response-time model. Held to ±4 seconds, so no variant is quietly longer to read.

      Pre-calibration on a live cohort, continuous re-estimation during delivery, and a post-hoc DIF sweep across language, region and gender. Read the equivalence specification →

      09 — Governance DPDP · GDPR

      Capture the minimum. Keep it briefly. Prove you deleted it.

      STAGE 01 · CAPTURE

      Purpose-bound

      Media is captured only for declared exam-integrity purposes, only for consented items, and only between session start and submission.

      STAGE 02 · MINIMISE

      Discard on close

      Unflagged video and audio are destroyed the moment the session seals. What survives is the event record — timestamps and outcomes, not footage.

      STAGE 03 · RETAIN

      30 days, encrypted

      Flagged clips only, encrypted at rest with per-session keys, accessible to assigned reviewers and to the candidate. Every access is logged to both.

      STAGE 04 · PURGE

      Automatic and evidenced

      Deletion runs on schedule, emits its own signed record, and pauses only for an open appeal. The candidate is notified when their file is gone.

      Residency

      All candidate data processed and stored in-country, on infrastructure the agency can inspect.

      Rights

      Access, correction, portability and grievance redressal wired into the candidate's own report view.

      Audit

      Append-only ledger of every verdict, access and purge, exportable for statutory audit.

      Independence

      Reviewer pool contracted separately from the delivery institution, with rotation and blind assignment.
